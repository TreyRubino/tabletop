# Session Three — The Long Road to Axeholm

Indented notes are the DM's alone, and each one is a place to stop reading.
**A ↳ heading is a condition — read what follows it only if that happens, and where two sit together read one side, not both. Everything else is read aloud.**

---

You come up out of that bowl in the hills in the first grey of the morning after, and behind you the shaft has filled itself in, and the hole in the hillside that Don-Jon's people spent two seasons opening is a dimple in the snow with a thread of grit still leaning away east on the wind.

Dazlyn has her rubbings. Tam has his cart. Don-Jon has a claim that is now a hill.

And Levi has a sleeve tied off above the wrist, and a right hand that will not stop opening and closing.

> - [ ] The shape of the night: the road home the long way, the farmhouse frozen solid, a cave with somebody in it, Barthen pays, the gnomes send Levi an arm, a night in town, then the Cragmaws and the doors of Axeholm. The arm and the correspondence at the castle are the two things that have to land.

# One — The Long Way Down

Nobody suggests going back the way you came, and nobody says why, and the track that runs south-west off the claim road is in better repair than it has any right to be — an old charcoal run, two ruts and a crown of frozen grass, bending down out of the hills toward the pine country instead of straight across the open.

It adds half a day. Not one of you argues about it.

The hills let you down slowly. The wind drops as the ground falls, and for the first hour you can still hear the hillside settling behind you, a long way off, like somebody moving furniture in another house. Then the pines come up around the track and take the sound away, and the world closes down to a corridor of black trunks and grey light and the ringing of four sets of hooves on ground too hard to hold a print.

It is colder than it was on the way out. Not a little. The puddles in the ruts are not iced over — they are ice all the way down, and the ice has gone white and bulged up out of the ruts in domes, the way water does when there is no liquid left under it to give.

> - [ ] Open road, no threat, and the first hour is theirs. Levi's arm, Caelus, the thing they all watched go through a wall — this is where the party talks about it if they are going to, and it is the only unhurried stretch in the session.

Somewhere in the second hour Kaelen says the thing that has been bothering her since the treeline.

**KAELEN:** "There's nothing out here."

And once she has said it you all notice it at the same time. No birds. No squirrel-chatter, no crows arguing in the tops, no rustle in the deadfall. A pine wood in winter is a quiet place and it is not a silent one, and this is silent, and it has been silent for a mile.

#### ↳ If they hunt

It takes Kaelen most of an hour to find anything at all, and what she finds is a hare.

It is sitting in the open in the middle of the afternoon, which hares do not do, and it does not run until she is close enough to see its eye. When she takes it, it weighs about half what a hare that size should weigh, and when she opens it there is nothing in the gut but pine needles and bark.

It was not hiding from her. It was too tired to.

> - [ ] One hare between four people. Enough for a pot and not enough for a meal, and the point of the scene is the gut full of bark. Anyone who wants to roll for more finds nothing, and finding nothing is the correct result.

#### ↳ If they do not hunt

You have Barthen's travelling rations and you are glad of them, and about mid-afternoon you pass the only living thing you see all day: a hare, sitting out in the open where no hare sits, too slow off the mark to bother chasing, its ribs showing through the winter coat.

The light goes early and it goes fast, and by the time the track climbs back up out of the pines you have perhaps an hour of grey left and the temperature is falling in a way you can feel on your teeth.

Then the trees end, and the open ground comes back, and there on the rise ahead of you, exactly where you left it, is the steading you sheltered in on the first night of the ride east.

Something is wrong with it from four hundred yards.

# Two — The Frozen House

At four hundred yards it is only that the house is the wrong colour. You slept on its floor four nights ago. You remember a low grey cottage behind a drystone wall, cold and long empty and dry, which was all any of you wanted of it. What is standing on the rise now is white — not snow-covered white, because the drifts around it are the ordinary blue-shadowed grey of old snow, but white like something glazed.

At a hundred yards you can see that it is shining.

Up close it is this.

The well has burst. The stone collar of it has come apart in four pieces and the water that came out has gone across the yard in a frozen sheet a foot thick at the lip and thinner as it spreads, and it has gone **in** — under the door, up the step, through the gap you all walked through on the way east. The ice has filled the house the way wax fills a mould. It stands in the doorway in a clear bulging wall you can put your hand on. It has come out of the one window at the front and hangs down the wall outside in a column with the shutter still inside it, open, held in the position somebody left it in.

It is not frosted and it is not rimed. It is clear. You can see into it for two or three feet before the light gives out, and what you can see is the inside of a house.

The hearth. The pale rectangles on the boards. The four hooks by the door.

And low on the doorpost, under perhaps two inches of absolutely clear ice, the column of knife-scratches. A child's height, marked off year on year, the whole run of them, with the notch at the top where somebody cut the last one out of the wood and took the piece away with them.

> - [ ] This is where the party spent the first night of the ride east — a day out of Phandalin, abandoned long before this winter, and they slept on its floor because it had a roof. Session two never played that night out, so whatever they remember of the inside of it is theirs. The recognition does the work and nothing needs explaining over it.

Amryn — you are the only one standing here who has ever had to make ice on purpose.

It is a first-year exercise. You did it in a bowl in a cold room with a ring chalked round it, and the thing they teach you before they teach you anything else is that the water has to give up its air, and the air takes time, and that is why ice is white. Cloudy ice is slow ice. Clear ice is ice that happened faster than the air inside it could get out.

You have never seen clear ice outside a laboratory. You have certainly never seen a house full of it.

Whatever came through here did in under a minute what the conclave needed a chalked ring and four hours to do to a bowl of water, and it did it to a building, in passing, on its way somewhere else.

The crust comes away in flakes, and underneath it the ice is like a flawed pane of glass, and when somebody holds the lamp flat against it the light goes in a foot and a half and keeps going.

You are looking into the room you slept in.

It is empty. Not wrecked and not abandoned in a hurry — empty the way a room is empty when somebody has swept it, which is how you found it four nights ago. The hearth, swept. The pale rectangles on the boards. The four hooks by the door with nothing on any of them.

And nobody in it. No shape on the floor, no shape in the chair, nothing at the window with its hands up.

> - [ ] That is the whole of the reward and for some parties it is worth a quarter of an hour: whatever came through here did not come through on top of anybody. There is nothing to carry out and no way to reach it. Mazh tells them tonight what the people who left this house walked into.

And then the cold arrives properly.

It does not blow in. There is no wind at all. It is a decision about whether to be alive in the morning.

> - [ ] The temperature falls off a cliff at dusk and keeps falling. No fire lit in the open will hold against it — anybody who tries gets a fire that burns with a small blue flame and throws no heat at all, which is worth describing. They need to be out of the air.

# Three — The Scrape

The cut in the hillside is a quarry mouth about the width of a cart, with a lip of frozen spoil below it and a lintel of undressed stone across the top that somebody put there a very long time ago and never came back to finish.

Twenty feet in it turns, and the wind — the wind that is not blowing anywhere outside — stops entirely, and the air goes from killing to merely cold in the space of two steps.

Forty feet in there is a fire.

It is very small. It is three sticks and a handful of pine cones in a ring of stones that have been used for this a great many times, and crouched over it with both hands almost in the flame there is an orc.

He is old. You do not often get to say that about an orc. The tusks are worn down and yellow and one of them is broken off short, the grey is through everything, and the hide of his shoulders hangs the way hide hangs on something that used to carry more. There is a bedroll, a cook pot, and a spear leaning against the wall eight feet away from him, well out of reach, and he does not look at it when you come round the corner.

He looks at you for a while. Then he goes back to looking at the fire.

**THE ORC:** "There is room."

> - [ ] He is not a fight and he will not become one. He is finished, he knows it, and he has decided where. If the party attack him he does not defend himself, and that is a thing they will carry.

> - [ ] Nothing later in the session needs him alive. If he is dead, or if nobody thinks to ask him anything, Kaelen picks the quarry tracks up off the ground outside the cut in the morning without a roll — they are the only worn thing on that hillside. The road to Phandalin is never gated on Mazh.

He speaks Common slowly and well, the way somebody does who learned it from traders over forty years and has had nobody to practise on lately. He will share the fire, and he will tell them anything they ask, and he has no interest at all in lying.

#### ↳ If they ask who he is

**THE ORC:** "Mazh. Of nothing, now. Of a place three days north that does not have anybody in it."

#### ↳ If they ask what he is doing here

**THE ORC:** "Waiting.

My people went south nine days ago. I went with them for two and then I sat down, and they argued about it, and I won the argument because I have been winning arguments with them for a long time.

A thing that walks slow at the back of a column kills the column. So." *He turns a stick in the fire.* "I came up here out of the air. There are worse rooms."

#### ↳ If they ask what happened north

**THE ORC:** "Cold.

Not winter. We know winter, we live in it, we are very good at it. This came in the second week of the month and it came at night and the first thing we knew of it was that the stream had stopped — not frozen over, *stopped*, standing up in the air in a shape like water, and the fish in it still in the shape of swimming.

Nineteen of us in the morning out of forty-one. The rest were where they slept.

So we went. Everything that lives up there went. You have been in the wood. You have seen."

#### ↳ If they ask whether he saw it

**THE ORC:** "I saw what it left.

My grandfather's word for it was *the white quiet*. I thought that was a story for children, and I have thought about that a great deal in the last seven days.

Something is on that mountain, and it is not sitting still on it any more, and it is coming down the way weather comes down." *He looks up at them for the first time since they sat.* "You are going north."

#### ↳ If they say they are going to Axeholm

**THE ORC:** "The dwarf fort with the shut doors.

Good. It is stone and it is deep and it has one mouth. If I were nineteen people I would be in there and not in a ditch south of here." *A long pause.* "I told them that. They wanted the coast."

Emeric — you have spent your whole life working out what a room wants in the first ten seconds, and this one is very simple.

He has not spoken to another living soul in seven days. He is answering every question put to him carefully and completely, in a language he learned off traders forty years ago and has had nobody to practise on, and he is answering them *slowly*.

He is not being evasive. He is making it last.

> - [ ] If Emeric plays, Mazh does not thank him and does not comment. He stops turning the stick in the fire and he does not start again until it is finished, and he asks for one more, and that is the whole of his reaction and it is enough.

> - [ ] Open floor at the fire. This is a long sit and it should be — no rolls needed for any of it, nobody has to persuade him of anything, and what the party decide to do about him is the whole scene.

> - [ ] Mazh's band and the six orcs the party killed on the road east are the same people. Forty-one went south. Nineteen saw the morning. Six of them turned back north up that road and met four riders coming the other way, and two of those six were the small ones being hurried along between the others. He does not know any of that, and nothing in this scene tells him unless the party choose to.

> - [ ] The carved horse from the archer's pack is the key to it and the party may not have it — it was in the pack in session two but it was never in the loot note, so it is just as likely still lying in the snow east of here. If they do not have it, none of what follows happens and the scene is simply a long sad conversation with a man who is waiting to die.

#### ↳ If anybody shows him the carved horse

He takes it in both hands. He turns it over once. He puts his thumb on the place where the paint has gone off the nose, which is exactly where a thumb would go.

Then he says a name. It is not in Common and he does not translate it.

**MAZH:** "Her father carried this. He would not put it anywhere he could not feel it."

He looks up, and he looks at the four of you, and you can watch him do the arithmetic — the road you came down, the day you came down it, how many of you there are, how well armed.

**MAZH:** "Where did you — "

And then he stops, with his mouth still open, and he closes it.

**MAZH:** "No. I have decided I do not want it."

He holds the horse out until somebody takes it back. Then he puts both hands flat on his knees and looks into the fire, and he does not say anything else for a long time, and when he does speak again it is about the weather.

> - [ ] This is the hardest beat in the session. He works it out in under two seconds and then refuses to have it confirmed, because being certain would oblige him to do something about it and he has nothing left to do anything with. He does not attack, he does not accuse, and he does not forgive anybody, because nobody has asked him to.

#### ↳ If the party tell him anyway

He listens to the whole of it without interrupting once, which is worse than interrupting.

**MAZH:** "Six. Of the nineteen." *A long pause.* "They turned back. Why would they turn back."

Then he asks them to go and sit at the other end of the cave, and he says it without heat, the way you would ask somebody to move down a bench.

> - [ ] He will not fight and he will not travel with them after this. In the morning he is gone, with the spear left behind and the horse set upright on a stone by the dead fire, and if anybody tracks him he has gone north, uphill, which is the wrong way for a man who wants to live.

#### ↳ If they take him with them

He will not go to Phandalin — he is extremely clear that a town is the one place on this coast where he will be killed by somebody's cousin — but he will ride as far as the last ridge above the valley, and he is useful the whole of that day — he knows the quarry tracks, he knows what the cold does and how fast, and he is good company in a bad way.

> - [ ] Where he is during Scenes Five, Six and Seven: camped in the lee of the ridge above the town with a horse and four days of Barthen's rations, for the night and the day the party spend inside the palisade. He is still there when they come out. If they want him for the Cragmaw stops he rides with them, and he is worth an extra pair of eyes at the thorn screen and nothing at all in the fight, because he will not raise the spear again for anybody.

He parts from them at the Trail, going west, and whether he turns up again is open.

#### ↳ If they leave him

He thanks them for the company, which is worse than if he had asked them to stay. The fire is out when they look back from the mouth of the cut, and he has not moved.

> - [ ] Either way the night passes. Deep stone, a small fire, and in the morning the air outside is survivable again and the ice on the farmhouse has not melted a drop.

# Four — The Wolves

> - [ ] Optional, and the only fight available before the Cragmaws — nothing from the start of the session to Scene Eight has any combat in it but this, which is deliberate, because the cold is the antagonist of the first half and the arm is the payoff. If the table starts looking for something to hit, this is where it goes, on the morning after the quarry. If they are engaged and happy, cut it and nothing is lost.

The morning after the scrape, on open ground with most of a day still to run, you come over a long rise and there are wolves on the track.

Nine of them. Eight of them are sitting down.

That is the first thing wrong with it. A pack that has seen four riders and a string of horses does not sit in the open at sixty yards and watch you come. They are strung out across the track in a loose line with their heads up, and the big one in the middle is standing, and none of them has made a sound.

The second thing wrong with it is how they look. You can count ribs at sixty yards. The winter coats have gone patchy and staring, there is one dragging a back leg that froze and did not come back, and the big one's muzzle is grey and scarred and it is the only one of the nine that looks like it has eaten this month.

They are not hunting you. They are between you and the only track south and they have nowhere else to be.

# Five — Barthen's

A day of hard travelling south-west on an old orc's quarry tracks, and then the hills open and the palisade of Phandalin is sitting in its valley with smoke standing straight up out of forty chimneys, and the smell of the place reaches you before the gate does — woodsmoke and wet wool and the tannery, and after four days of it being somewhere else, it smells like the best thing that has ever happened.

Barthen's Provisions is the long low building on the south side of the square with the door propped despite the weather, and Elmar Barthen is behind the counter writing in a ledger, and he keeps writing for exactly as long as it takes him to finish the sentence before he looks up.

Then he sees the four of you, and then he sees Levi's sleeve, and he puts the pen down.

**BARTHEN:** "The lad rode in this morning on one of Don-Jon's ponies and I have had the whole of it twice already. Once from him and once from the stable boy, who has improved it."

He comes round the counter.

**BARTHEN:** "He is in the kitchen out the back because he will not stop talking and his mother has had enough of it. Go through if you like. He has told everybody in this town that you came down a hole after him and he has not once got the order of events right."

He looks at the sleeve again, and does not look away from it, and does not ask.

**BARTHEN:** "And the cart is in the yard. Tam brought it down with Don-Jon's people behind him and the near-side wheel is cracked through two spokes and the tarp is gone and there is a quantity of hill in the bed of it that I will be shovelling out until spring.

I want to be clear with you about something, because you have come in here with one arm fewer than you went out with and I am not going to let this get muddled.

I asked you to bring my cart back. I asked that second." *He puts his hand flat on the counter.* "Nineteen years old, thin, talks too much, works for me. That is what I asked for first and that is what is sitting in my kitchen eating everything in it.

So the job is done. Do not let anybody tell you otherwise, and do not let me hear that you have been going round this town counting what it cost you."

> - [ ] Barthen's commission from session two was the lad first and the cart second, and this is it being closed properly. The cart came down with Don-Jon's party and needs a wheelwright, which is Barthen's problem and not the party's. If anybody tries to apologise for the state of it he talks over them.

> - [ ] Barthen's rumour is that he fronts supplies on a promise and nobody knows how he stays solvent. The account is open, it is real, and it is the only payment for the excavation job — the Cragmaw stops pay their own way later.

**BARTHEN:** "I said anything off these shelves, no bill, for as long as you are working out of this town, and I meant it, and I am not going to stand here and watch you be polite about it.

Here is the honest position. It is a bad winter and my shelves are not what they were, so what I have is what came in on the last cart and what I have been able to make or keep. Take what you want of it. When the roads open and more comes in, there will be different things, and the account does not run out."

# Six — The Package

The Stonehill Inn is the biggest building in Phandalin and the only one with two floors, and at this hour of the afternoon the taproom is four farmers, a dog, and a fire built properly.

Toblen Stonehill is behind the bar wiping a tankard that was clean when he picked it up. He is a slight, nervy, friendly man who talks fast and tops up a drink before you have asked, and when the four of you come through the door he puts the tankard down mid-wipe and says, to the room rather than to you —

**TOBLEN:** "Right. Out. All of you. Finish those outside or finish them tomorrow."

Nobody moves, because they have never heard him say anything like it, and then they all move at once.

## What the arm does

**THE LONG REACH.** A quarter turn and the hand comes off the wrist. It pays out on the chain to thirty feet and he moves it as though it were still on the end of his arm, because as far as he is concerned it is.

**The small work — at will, no action, once on his turn.** The hand does one plain physical thing within thirty feet: fetch, hold, open, carry, lift something off a floor he cannot reach, hold a door shut, keep a grip on a rope. This costs the spring nothing and he can do it all day.

**The hard use — as an action, and it spends the whole spring.** One of two things:

- **Take hold.** One creature he can see within thirty feet, Large or smaller. Dexterity saving throw, DC 8 + his proficiency bonus + his Strength modifier. On a failure the hand closes on it and it is **grappled**: speed 0, escape with an Athletics or Acrobatics check against the same DC as an action. The grip is a ratchet and holds without power, so it keeps holding after the spring is spent — it opens when the creature escapes, when Levi releases it, or when the chain is cut. **To drag it**, Levi hauls on the chain with his own arm: an Athletics check contested by the creature's Athletics or Acrobatics, as an action, moving it ten feet on a win.
- **Anchor and haul.** Stone or timber he can see within thirty feet. The hand sets, the chain goes taut, and he is pulled to within five feet of it. Forced movement, so it provokes nothing. Anchoring above himself leaves him hanging on the chain rather than falling, because the ratchet holds, and climbing the rest of it is ordinary Athletics.

**The chain.** AC 17, 10 hit points, severed at 0. It only takes damage from an attack aimed at it, and a creature held by the hand can reach the chain whatever the distance, because it is attached to it. The chain retracts by hand — he reels it in — so a spent spring never strands the hand out of reach. A severed chain means the hand is a loose brass object to be picked up and carried to somebody who can re-link it.

**The cost of the hard use.** The spring is spent, and until it is wound there is no second hard use and no small work either. Winding is eight turns of the key: he braces the cuff between his knees and turns the key with his right hand, which takes his **action on two consecutive turns** — about twelve seconds, in the open, in front of whatever he just did that to. He is holding nothing while he does it. If he takes damage mid-wind, DC 10 Constitution save, and on a failure the count starts again. He can wind it with the hand still out on the chain or still gripping something; what he cannot do is wind it and fight in the same breath.

**The cost of carrying it.** Wound, it ticks, and in a quiet room everybody can hear it — **disadvantage on his Stealth checks** unless he lets the spring run all the way down first, which leaves him with no hand and no small work. And when the spring goes, the ticking stops, which is a thing anything that has been listening to it will notice.

**The shield.** A shield is strapped to the forearm and gripped in the hand. The strap stays on the cuff; the grip does not. So while the hand is away on the chain, Levi has a shield hanging off his arm and nothing holding it — **no shield bonus to AC** until the hand comes back. A two-handed weapon is the same problem from the other end: he cannot two-hand anything while the hand is thirty feet away.

> - [ ] What this is: a grapple, a grapple gun and a third hand. The resource is the spring and nothing else — there is no per-rest limit to track, because winding it takes two actions in the middle of a fight and that is the limit. At this level the save lands around DC 15, which most things with a Dexterity of 14 fail about half the time; the Large cap is what keeps it off anything that matters later, not the DC. The twelve seconds of kneeling in the open winding his own arm is the whole design, and it is worth letting the table watch him do it.

> - [ ] On the party's spread: Levi is four or five levels above everybody else, and the arm widens that. The honest way to run it is to let him be the answer to the thing in front of them and then put the next thing somewhere his arm cannot reach — a locked door, a conversation, something that needs Kaelen to read ground or Amryn to read a page. Every fight in this session is built so the other three have something to do that is not damage.

Seven towers, and only the walls between them still agree on where they are. Six of the towers have fallen in on themselves and stand open to the sky like broken teeth. The curtain wall is down in three places and standing in four. A causeway runs in from the west across a ditch that has been dry for so long there are full-grown pines in the bottom of it, and at the end of the causeway there is a gatehouse with both gates gone and a passage through it you could ride four abreast.

It is all very obvious. It is the only way in that anybody would look at twice.

There is smoke coming out of one of the towers.

It is not the tower the causeway goes to.

### What is on them

The hobgoblin is the one worth standing over.

The mail shirt has been **fitted** — taken in at the waist and lengthened through the arm by somebody who had this creature stand still and be measured, and that is not a thing that happens to a hobgoblin in a ruin in the Neverwinter Wood. And folded into the breast of it, gone soft from being carried about, there is a half-sheet of good pale paper with a drill rota written on it in a fine even hand. Four names. Four posts. The hours of the watch.

Somebody wrote this thing a training schedule.

The goblins have less and it is still too much. Tokens on cords, the same as the ones in the hole. A carter's leather glove off a Lionshield cart with the hart stamped into the cuff. Between the two of them, about as much coin as a farmhand in Phandalin sees in a season.

### Grol

The lived-in tower is three floors of which two are a floor, and the top one has been made into something.

There is a chair in it. Somebody has carried a high-backed wooden chair up two flights of broken stair and set it at the far end of the room with a bearskin over it, and there is a stand beside it with a cup on it, and a brazier, and a rug that came off a cart.

In the chair there is a bugbear the size of a door, in a brass-studded leather coat that fits him, and he is holding a cup he has not drunk out of, and he does not get up.

**GROL:** "You are the ones from the hole in the hill."

> - [ ] He knows. Word came up the Trail with the carts, two days behind the party, and he has had about a day to think about it.

**GROL:** "Grol. King of the Cragmaws, which is seven towers and thirty goblins and a wood nobody wants.

Sit down if you like. There is one chair." *He drinks, finally, and makes a face at it.* "You have been through two of my camps in two days and I am going to be honest with you about how much that bothers me, which is not very much. They were not good at it."

#### ↳ If they ask about the toll

**GROL:** "It works. Does it not?

Six months and not one dead driver and not one burned cart and not one soldier out of Neverwinter looking for us. You go up and down that Trail and you take a *piece*, and you give them a token, and they pay it again next month because paying is easier.

Before that we raided. Do you know how many of mine died raiding? I do. I have the sticks."

#### ↳ If they ask whose idea it was

He looks at the cup for a while.

**GROL:** "Not mine. I will not pretend it was mine."

#### ↳ If they push him on who

**GROL:** "There is one who comes. Every month, near enough, and never twice on the same day of it.

Takes the sticks. Reads them — reads them, in front of me, which is a thing I have thought about. Leaves the tokens. Takes the share. Tells me what the rate will be. Has never once raised a voice at me and I would rather be shouted at.

I do not know a name. I have never seen a face. The hood does not come off and I have stopped hoping it will."

#### ↳ If they ask why he goes along with it

**GROL:** "Because the first time it came, it came alone, through a wood full of my people, and nobody saw it come in.

You ask me why. That is why."

Emeric — he is doing something you have watched a hundred men do across a table, and it is this.

He is not negotiating and he is not stalling. He has worked out that there is no version of the next ten minutes where he comes out of this a king, and he has decided that what he wants instead is for somebody else to be the one who has to deal with the thing that comes every month.

He is trying to hand you a problem. He has been sitting in that chair for four days working out how to make it sound like an offer.

> - [ ] If Emeric lets him, Grol gives up everything in the room without a single roll — the letters, the schedule, the tokens, where the carts go and when the next visit is due. He will not stop talking once he starts. What he wants in exchange is to be somewhere else by the thaw, and he will say so out loud if anybody asks him what he wants.

# Ten — The Doors

Two days north-west out of the wood on a trail that nobody has used since autumn, and the cold comes back as you climb, and by the second morning your water is freezing in the skins inside your packs.

Icespire Peak is on your right hand the whole way and it does not look like a mountain with weather on it any more. It looks like a mountain that is *making* weather, and the grey of it is down off the summit and spread along the shoulders and reaching out over the foothills in a flat shelf you can see the edge of.

Then the trail turns the side of a hill, and the hillside ahead of you is not a hillside.

It is a wall.

Dwarven work, forty feet of it, cut back into the slope and dressed square, with a shelf of flat ground in front of it under a foot of unbroken snow. Two hundred years of weather have not taken a corner off it. There are no bodies, no rubble, no scorch, no sign that anybody ever fought here at all.

And in the middle of the wall there is a gate, and the gate is shut.

Both leaves. Flush. Undamaged. The seam between them packed solid with two centuries of ice, and the hinge-pins sound, and the lintel unbroken, and not one mark on the whole face of it that anybody made from this side.
