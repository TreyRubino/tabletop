# Session Three — Image Prompts

For ChatGPT image generation. Paste the **Style Block** once at the top of a conversation, then paste prompts one at a time. Every prompt is written to stand alone, so you can also paste any single one together with the Style Block.

---

## Style Block

> **Hold this style for every image in this set.**
>
> Photorealistic cinematic fantasy. Shot as if on a full-frame camera with a 35mm lens at f/2.8 — real depth of field, real atmosphere, visible air. Natural light only: overcast daylight, firelight, lamplight, or moonlight. No stylisation, no cel shading, no anime, no painterly brushwork, no comic-book linework, no glow effects that a camera could not capture.
>
> Palette: desaturated winter. Slate greys, blue shadow on snow, black-green pine, wet stone, brass and iron. Warm light exists only where there is a visible source for it.
>
> Texture and wear are the point. Wool is pilled, leather is salt-stained and cracked, steel is scratched, snow is crusted and dirty at the edges rather than fresh. Faces are weathered, pores and stubble visible, breath fogging in cold air.
>
> Composition: single clear subject, generous negative space, horizon low. Think production still from a serious historical film, not a game cover. No text, no watermarks, no borders, no UI, no title cards.
>
> Aspect ratio 16:9 unless the prompt says otherwise.

## Character Reference

**You are supplying the PC portraits yourself — attach those images as reference whenever a prompt features one of them, and the text below is only a fallback for when you have not got a reference to hand.** Nothing in this file is a portrait prompt for the four of them; the standalone portraits here are all NPCs.

Paste the relevant lines into any prompt where that character appears, so they stay consistent across the set.

**LEVI WHITE** — Male high elf, early thirties in human terms. Tall, broad, straight-backed. Pale skin, dark hair cut short and practical. Gilded plate armour, scuffed and road-worn rather than parade-clean, over a padded gambeson. Hangs a two-handed greatsword across his back. **His left arm ends two inches below the elbow** — early images in this set show it as a tied-off sleeve over a bandaged stump; from the Stonehill Inn onward he wears a brass mechanical forearm. Carries himself like someone who was raised to be looked at.

**AMRYN VELARIS** — Female high elf, slight, sharp-featured, very pale. Long dark hair, usually unbound and untidy. Dark layered travelling robes, worn thin at the cuffs, with burn-marks on the forearms that have been patched over more than once. Stands at the edge of light rather than in it. Expression: assessing, impatient, about to try something.

**KAELEN THORNWOOD** — Female wood elf, lean and heavily built through the shoulders. Weathered brown skin, dark hair in a practical braid. Layered furs and oiled leather over a mail shirt, hood down. A double-headed axe across her back. Always scanning — in any group shot she is the one looking at the exits rather than the camera.

**EMERIC** — Male half-elf, mid-thirties in appearance. Road-stained brown leather, a lute slung on a frayed strap. Expressive hands, mid-gesture, mouth open mid-sentence. The only one in the group who looks like he is enjoying himself.

## 1 — Leaving the Collapsed Dig
*Opening shot. Scene One.*

> Wide establishing shot, low winter afternoon light under heavy overcast. A shallow bowl between two bare hills in snow country. In the centre of the bowl the ground has subsided — a wide dimple of collapsed earth and broken timber where a mine shaft has caved in, with a thin column of pale dust still rising from it and leaning sideways on the wind. Sorted spoil heaps along one edge, now partly fallen in. A single horse-cart and a canvas lean-to at the rim, tiny in frame. Four mounted figures on the track in the foreground, seen from behind, riding away from the collapse. No faces. Deep tyre-and-hoof ruts in dirty crusted snow. Desaturated slate and blue-grey palette.
>

## 2 — The Emptied Wood
*Scene One. The silence.*

> Eye-level shot down a corridor of old pine forest in winter. Black-brown trunks receding into grey haze, bare brown needle-litter floor, patches of crusted snow. Flat diffuse green-grey light coming down from a canopy far overhead. Absolutely no animals, no birds, no movement — the stillness is the subject. A narrow frozen cart-track running away from camera, the puddles in its ruts frozen solid and bulged up into white domes. In the middle distance, small in frame, a single mounted rider stopped and looking up into the canopy.
>
> [+ Style Block]

## 3 — The Frozen Farmhouse, Wide
*Scene Two. The big reveal of the session's first half.*

> Wide shot, last fifteen minutes of grey daylight. A small low stone cottage with a slate roof standing alone on a rise behind a drystone wall, in open snow country. **The entire house is encased in clear ice.** A burst stone well in the yard has flooded the ground in a glassy frozen sheet a foot thick at its lip, thinning as it spreads; the sheet runs under the front door and up the step. Ice fills the doorway in a clear bulging wall. A column of ice hangs from the single front window down the outside wall with the wooden shutter frozen inside it, still held open. The ice is transparent, not frosted or rimed — faint interior shapes visible a few feet into it. The surrounding snow drifts are ordinary dirty grey-blue by contrast, which makes the house read as glazed and wrong. Mountain mass just visible far behind under a flat grey cloud shelf.
>
> [+ Style Block]

## 4 — The Doorpost Under the Ice
*Scene Two. The gut-punch. Shoot this tight.*

> Extreme close-up, shallow depth of field, cold blue-grey light. A weathered wooden doorpost seen through roughly two inches of perfectly clear ice. Cut into the wood beneath the ice is a vertical column of small knife-scratches — a child's height marked off year after year, a dozen or more notches with faint illegible marks beside them. At the top of the column, a clean rectangular gouge where the uppermost mark has been cut out of the wood and removed. Ice surface catching a little light; grain of the wood sharp and legible through it. No people in frame. Vertical 4:5 crop.
>

## 5 — The Cloud Coming Off the Mountain
*Scene Two. Transition shot — the dragon thread, never showing the dragon.*

The edge of the shelf is distinct enough to trace. Clear bitter sky above it, hazy dark country below it. Enormous sense of scale. No creature visible anywhere.

## 6 — Mazh at the Fire

## 7 — Phandalin from the Ridge

Hills closing it in on three sides, pine country beyond. Warm window-light in a few buildings against a cold blue-grey world. Hopeful, small, besieged by weather.

## 8 — Barthen's Provisions

## 9 — The Arm
*Scene Five. The centrepiece of the session. Worth generating several times.*

> Product-still composition on dark wool, lit by a single oil lamp from the upper left. A complete mechanical left forearm and hand in unpolished brass, severed cleanly two inches below where an elbow would be. Finished rather than shiny — soft dull warm patina, the surface of something made to be handled for decades. Fully articulated: every joint a human hand has, plus two extra articulations in the wrist. Fingers very slightly longer than human. Palm is a single smooth plate. Exposed clockwork visible through cut-away panels along the forearm, including a coiled spring housing the size of a fist. A small folding key set flush into the outside of the elbow housing. A fine brass chain just visible coiled inside the forearm cavity. A small engraved brass maker's plate on the inner forearm. Deep shadow around it, dust motes in the lamp beam. Beautiful, heavy, obviously expensive, obviously a gift. Vertical 4:5 crop.
>
> [+ Style Block]

## 10 — The Long Reach
*Scene Five. Showing the spell, for later use in a fight.*

> Low-angle action shot in dim stone interior, lamplight. A brass mechanical hand in mid-air roughly fifteen feet from its wearer, fingers spread and closing, trailing a taut fine brass chain back to the open wrist socket of an armoured figure's left arm. Motion blur on the chain only; the hand itself sharp. The wearer braced, right shoulder forward, looking along the chain. Dust and grit disturbed in the air. Reads as mechanical and physical, not magical — no glow, no particles, no energy effects. The chain catching one hard highlight from the lamp.
>

On the main table a small frost-blue scale on a plaited cord being held up to the candlelight by someone, with three faces turned toward it. Deep warm orange light, heavy shadow, smoke haze in the roof space. Noisy, close, safe.

## 14 — The Customs House
*Scene Seven. The thing that matters in the hideout.*

> Interior of a rough cave chamber lit by one hanging oil lamp. A plank laid across two barrels serving as a desk; an iron-bound strongbox beside it on the floor. Everything squalid except the record-keeping, which is immaculate — that contrast is the subject of the image. Smoke-blackened ceiling, damp rock, lamp-glow falling off fast into dark.

## 15 — Cragmaw Castle
*Scene Eight. Establishing shot.*

> Wide shot, late afternoon, deep old pine forest in winter, flat green-grey light failing early. ## 16 — Grol's Throne Room
*Scene Eight. Character and set.*

> Interior, top floor of a ruined stone tower, lit by a brazier and one shuttered window. Broken flagstone floor, one wall partly open to the weather with snow drifted in across the stones. At the far end, a high-backed wooden dining chair — plainly carried up here from somewhere else — draped with a bearskin, set up as a throne. A looted carpet unrolled in front of it. In the chair sits an enormous bugbear, head and shoulders above a tall man, in a brass-studded leather coat that actually fits him, holding a pewter cup he is not drinking from. He is not posturing; he looks like a man in a job he did not apply for. Against the side wall a trestle table with an oil lamp, an inkhorn, a dozen cut quills and a weighted stack of pale rag paper — conspicuously out of place in the room. Firelight orange, window light cold blue, long shadows.

## 17 — The Hand on the Paper
*Scene Eight. The thread. Shoot it tight and cold.*

> Extreme close-up, lamplight, shallow focus. Two sheets of good pale rag paper overlapping on a rough plank table. Both are covered in dense, fast, confident handwriting in black iron-gall ink — fine-nibbed, perfectly even, not one hesitation or correction anywhere, the hand of someone writing something they already know. Columns of figures down one margin. Beside the papers, a cut quill and a ruling pen. The paper is clean and expensive and the table it is lying on is filthy and splintered, and that contrast is the subject. No readable words — render the writing as convincing illegible script.
>
> [+ Style Block]

## 18 — The Doors of Axeholm
*Scene Nine. Final image of the session. Make this the best one.*

> Wide, slightly low-angle hero shot in hard cold morning light. A sheer dressed-stone dwarven fortress wall forty feet high, cut back directly into a steep mountainside so the hill becomes architecture — massive precise blocks, no mortar line visible, not one corner weathered off in two centuries. A shelf of flat ground in front of it under a foot of absolutely unbroken snow. Set into the centre of the wall, a pair of enormous iron-bound stone gates, **shut flush and completely undamaged** — no breach, no scorching, no rubble, no sign anyone ever fought here. The seam between the two leaves packed solid with centuries of grey ice. Four small figures and their horses at the bottom of the frame for scale, one of them a short broad figure with both hands flat against the gate. Snow-covered peak visible above and behind with a grey cloud shelf spreading off it. Immense, sealed, patient, and entirely silent.
>
> [+ Style Block]

## 19 — The Wolves on the Rise
*Scene Three and a Half. The standoff, before anybody decides anything.*

> Wide shot along a frozen cart track over open rolling snow country, flat grey morning light. Across the track at roughly sixty yards, nine wolves strung out in a loose line — most of them sitting, one large grey-muzzled male standing in the centre. They are badly starved: ribs and hips visible, winter coats gone patchy and staring, one dragging a useless back leg. Two are clearly half-grown and the thinnest of the group. Heads up, ears forward, utterly silent and not advancing. Foreground, out of focus, the heads and shoulders of two horses and a rider's gloved hand on a rein. The wolves are pitiful rather than frightening and the composition should make that unmistakable. Breath fogging from both sides.
>
> [+ Style Block]

## 20 — The Dam Goes
*Scene Seven. The clever solution.*

> Action shot up a narrow steep-sided woodland defile. A crude dam of stones, brush and packed mud forty feet above a cave mouth has just been breached — the near side collapsing outward, brown water and ice-slush bursting through the gap and dropping down the defile in a chute. Spray and torn brush in the air. A figure braced on the slope above with a crowbar, leaning back away from the water. Below, the dark cave mouth is already swallowing the flood. Fast shutter, water frozen in mid-collapse, every droplet sharp. Cold flat daylight, no sun.
>
> [+ Style Block]

Two smaller goblin figures being carried past it by the current in the background, arms up, already going. Chaotic, loud, close quarters.

## 22 — The Bugbear from Behind
*Scene Seven. The ambush, if they go in the front.*

> Tight interior shot, narrow cave passage, one lamp on the floor out of frame throwing light upward. Foreground right: the shoulder and back of a traveller's cloak, out of focus, unaware. Mid-ground centre: a bugbear emerging from a side cleft in the rock that was not visible a second ago — enormous, hunched to fit the passage, moving fast and low with a club already back, lips drawn off the teeth, eyes catching the lamplight. Depth of field shallow so the threat is sharp and the victim is soft. Claustrophobic framing, rock close on both sides. The subject is the ambush, not the monster.

## 23 — The Killing Box
*Scene Eight. What the front door actually is.*

> Shot from inside a ruined stone gatehouse passage looking out toward daylight, deep shadow in the foreground. The passage is wide, swept clean and inviting, with bright snow and the causeway visible at the far end. In the dark of the foreground, three crude firing positions have been built into the broken masonry above and beside the passage — stacked stone breastworks with arrow slits and sighting notches, each one angled to cover the others. A goblin crouched in the nearest one with a shortbow across its knees, back to camera, watching the bright end. The point of the image is that the inviting way in is a trap and the trap was designed by somebody competent.

## 24 — The Drill in the Yard
*Scene Eight. The tell.*

> Mid shot in a snow-covered ruined castle courtyard under flat green-grey forest light. A hobgoblin — tall, orange-skinned, straight-backed, in a mail shirt that visibly fits properly — standing square with a drill-stick, facing two goblins who are attempting to hold a shield-and-spear line and are extremely bad at it: out of step, spears crossed, one looking at the other instead of forward. The hobgoblin's posture is a parade-ground instructor's. Broken towers and open sky behind, trodden snow and old needle-litter underfoot. The comedy and the menace are in the same frame and both should read.
>
> [+ Style Block]

## 25 — The Yard Fight
*Scene Eight. The main engagement.*

> Wide low action shot in a ruined castle courtyard, late afternoon forest light, trodden grey snow. A melee of six or seven figures spread across the frame rather than clustered: a tall armoured figure in scuffed gilded plate mid-swing with a greataxe-sized two-handed sword, a fur-clad elf driving a double-headed axe into a goblin's shield, goblins breaking in two directions, and a hobgoblin at the far side still holding formation alone with its shield up and its spear level. Broken masonry and fallen roof-beams as cover throughout. Snow and needle-litter kicked up, breath and steam in the air, blood minimal and dark. Real weight in every pose, no heroic posing, no motion streaks.
>
> [+ Style Block]

## 26 — Winding It in the Open
*Scene Eight or any later fight. The cost of the arm.*

> Mid shot, lit by late low sun through broken stone. An armoured figure in scuffed gilded plate standing in the open in a ruin, head down, using his right hand to crank a small folding brass key set into the elbow housing of his brass mechanical left forearm. The hand at the end of it hangs completely dead and limp on its chain. His greatsword is on the ground at his feet. In the near background, soft-focus, two more figures still fighting. Utterly exposed and visibly mid-task. The subject is vulnerability, not power.

# Notes on using these

**Order of priority if you only generate a few.** 3 (the frozen house), 9 (the arm), 18 (the doors), 4 (the doorpost), 15 (the castle), 25 (the yard fight). Those six carry the session.

**Where each one goes.** Opening 1. Scene One 2. Scene Two 3, 4, 5. Scene Three 6, 29. Scene Three and a Half 19. Scene Four 7, 8. Scene Five 9, 10, 27. Scene Six 11, 12, 28. Scene Seven 13, 14, 20, 21, 22. Scene Eight 15, 16, 17, 23, 24, 25, 26. Scene Nine 18.

**Battles are 19 to 26.** Pull 20 or 21 if they cut the dam, 22 if they walk in the front. 23 before the castle approach, 24 and 25 during it. 26 is reusable in every fight from here on, because the arm has to be wound in the open every single time.

**Character consistency.** Generate each NPC once, keep the best result, and feed it back as a reference image for any later prompt they appear in. 6, 12, 16, 27, 28 are built as standalone portraits for exactly that.

**If a result comes back stylised**, the usual culprits are the words *fantasy*, *magical* and *glowing*. Re-prompt with "documentary photograph, no stylisation" added to the front and drop any adjective that implies a genre.

**Faces.** 1, 4, 5, 10, 17, 18 deliberately avoid or obscure faces, which keeps them reusable even if the party roster changes.
